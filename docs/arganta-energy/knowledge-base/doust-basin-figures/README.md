# Doust "Dissecting Sedimentary Basins" — figure sourcing map

Purpose: before extracting any actual image bytes from the source booklet, determine —
for each of its 49 figures — whether it is Doust's own original compilation (no
external source named) or explicitly reproduced/adapted from a named external work.
This table is the input to the permission conversation: figures in the "Own" column are
what to ask Doust about directly; figures in the "External (named)" column are ultimately
someone else's IP (a different author/publisher), citation notwithstanding.

**Source**: the complete PDF (`Dissecting Sedimentary Basins by Harry Doust.pdf`, 102 pages,
owner `webmaster@kngmg.nl` — the Royal Netherlands Geological and Mining Society) was
supplied directly and kept as a local working reference at
`docs/arganta-energy/knowledge-base/doust-basin-figures/_source/` — **gitignored, never
committed**, since it is third-party copyrighted material. Every figure's full caption and
References section was extracted from that PDF directly (text only), closing an earlier gap
where a partial text extraction cut off mid-page 79.

**No image files are stored in this folder.** Per copyright policy, image extraction is
deliberately held back pending the outcome of asking Doust for permission on his own figures.
This README is a text-only reference map.

## Legend

- **Own** — no external work named anywhere in the caption or immediately-surrounding text; appears to be Doust's own sketch/compilation (often of a real, named basin).
- **External (named)** — caption explicitly says "from", "modified from", "adapted from", "redrawn from", "based on", or "simplified from" a specific named source. That source's author/publisher — not Doust — holds rights to the original.
- **Compiled (unnamed)** — caption says it is compiled/interpreted from multiple sources but does not name them.

## Notes for the permission conversation

- Figures attributed to *Sedimentary Basins of US + Canada* (2019, ed. Miall, Elsevier) — figs 36, 37 — are book-chapter reproductions; permission there would run through Elsevier/the chapter author, not Doust, even though Doust cites the page numbers precisely.
- **Figs 46 and 47 are a special case**: their named sources are Doust's own earlier co-authored papers (Doust & Sumner 2007; Beglinger, Corver, Doust, Cloetingh & Thurmond 2012). Doust may hold or share rights to the originals — worth asking him about these two specifically, separately from the true third-party figures.
- The "Own" figures are frequently annotated cross-sections of specific real, named basins (Viking Graben, Williston, Arabian Basin, Orange Basin, etc.) that Doust appears to have drawn himself from public geological knowledge of those basins — these are the strongest candidates for "Doust made it himself."
- Fig. 1 and 27, while adapted/annotated from a named external classification/framework, still involve Doust's own drafting — worth clarifying with him directly whether he considers the drawing itself his own IP layered on someone else's classification/data.
- Fig. 6's source (Wikipedia) carries a CC-BY-SA license — a different, likely simpler permission path than the academic sources, but attribution requirements still apply.
