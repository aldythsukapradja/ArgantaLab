# Arganta Fieldcraft Passport

Research benchmark, product spine and first-pathway proposal  
Status: proposal for founder review  
Date: 2026-08-02

## Executive decision

Build **Arganta Fieldcraft Passport** as an evidence-native learning and competency system inside ArgantaEnergy, not as a folder of presentations and not as a generic LMS. It must be one reusable training product with two equal delivery modes:

- **Online Passport** — self-paced, resumable, individually assessed and credentialed.
- **Fieldcraft Live** — instructor-led enterprise delivery over 3–5 days, using the same content, app workspaces, data, question banks and credential rules.

The strongest model is a deliberate hybrid:

- **SLB NExT** for discipline progression, domain + software integration and learning by doing.
- **PetroSkills / IHRDC** for role-based competency maps, proficiency levels, diagnostics and gap-closing plans.
- **Coursera Professional Certificates** for the learner journey: clear course series, progress, hands-on work, final credential and shareable proof.
- **SEG CMS** for connecting self-assessment, job profiles and recommended learning resources.
- **Open Badges 3.0 / CLR 2.0** for portable, verifiable credentials rather than an image-only certificate.
- **Arganta's unique advantage**: every learning claim, worked example, lab, answer explanation and credential can point to governed app evidence, its truth class and its source version.

The recommended brand architecture is:

> **ARGANTA FIELDCRAFT** — the academy and enterprise-training product  
> **Fieldcraft Passport** — the learner's competency and credential record  
> **The Volve Mission: From Discovery to Decision** — the first end-to-end program  
> **One field. Every lifecycle. Decisions proven by evidence.** — campaign line

The first flagship should be:

> **The Volve Mission: From Discovery to Decision**  
> Follow one real North Sea field across the full upstream lifecycle, operate the actual Arganta workspaces and earn the **Integrated Geoscience Fieldcraft Passport — Foundation**.

This should be the first layer of a larger passport system spanning Geology, Geophysics, Petrophysics, Reservoir Engineering, Well Delivery, Reservoir Management, Data and Energy Transition.

## What the research says

### Benchmark table

| Benchmark | Strongest pattern | What Arganta should adopt | What Arganta should improve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SLB NExT | Foundation → Skill → Advanced progression; domain, software and combined courses; hands-on projects and simulations | Four proficiency levels, integrated domain/workbench lessons, real project progression | Avoid vendor-software dependence; keep exercises evidence-portable and tool-transparent |
| PetroSkills | Competency maps across geology, geophysics, petrophysics, reservoir, drilling and data; four proficiency levels; recurring governance | Competency framework, role targets and scheduled content review | Make the map visible and motivating to the learner, not mainly an enterprise matrix |
| IHRDC | Assessment → gap → targeted learning plan → reassessment; competency models linked to content and reporting | Diagnostic assessment, adaptive pathway and manager/team reporting | Replace large passive catalogues with shorter case-driven missions |
| SEG CMS | Self-assessment against career models and recommendations that include courses, papers, books and on-job assignments | Mix formal learning with reading, evidence review and work assignments | Add actual in-app practice and evidence artifacts, not recommendations alone |
| Coursera | Course-series packaging, predictable module rhythm, progress, practice assessments, projects and recognizable completion credential | Familiar learner UX, resumability, deadlines optional, shareable completion | Make the credential prove specific competencies and evidence, not merely completion |
| Open Badges / CLR | Machine-verifiable achievement metadata, issuer, criteria, evidence, alignment and portability | Use Open Badges 3.0 for badges and CLR 2.0 for the whole passport record | Preserve human-readable PDF certificates as a presentation layer, not the source of truth |

### Primary benchmark findings

1. **Competency comes before content.** The catalogue should be generated from roles, skills and required proficiency—not from uploaded presentations.
2. **A pathway must mix theory and work.** NExT combines domain material, software workflows, projects, field/lab experiences and mentoring. In Arganta, the equivalent is concept lesson + real data lab + decision scenario + evidence review.
3. **Quizzes alone do not prove capability.** Multiple-choice assessments are appropriate, but they should ask learners to interpret real Arganta views, tables, plots and evidence rather than recall isolated facts.
4. **The final project should begin early.** Each module should produce one small artifact or decision that becomes part of the final Volve field synthesis.
5. **Credentials need verifiable criteria and evidence.** The certificate page should disclose what was completed, assessment version, score, competencies demonstrated and credential status.
6. **The content system must support revision.** Courses, assessments, sources and credentials require immutable version IDs so new data or corrected interpretations do not silently change what a learner was tested on.

## Product position

### Product promise

**One field. Every lifecycle. Decisions proven by evidence.**

The learner does not merely study how upstream work is performed. The learner enters a field mission, moves through the same lifecycle navigation as an asset team, uses the same Arganta components and engines as the production app, makes decisions and leaves with a verifiable record of what they demonstrated.

The Passport is simultaneously:

- a learner-facing academy;
- a 3–5 day instructor-led enterprise course;
- an individual competency record;
- a reusable course-authoring system;
- a governed bridge from Arganta Data + Knowledge to training;
- a team capability dashboard later;
- a verifiable credential issuer later.

It is not:

- a presentation viewer with a quiz attached;
- a replacement for accredited university education;
- a claim that MCQs alone certify field performance;
- an AI-generated content stream without expert review;
- a copy of Petrel, NExT, PetroSkills, IHRDC or Coursera.
- a separate simplified training application that duplicates Arganta's engines.

The creative theme is **an evidence-led field mission**, expressed as **mission control meets a geoscientist's field notebook**. It should feel technically serious, cinematic and memorable—not like a conventional corporate LMS.

## Navigation and number of tabs

### Shell recommendation

Add one first-class desktop sidebar group called **LEARN**, with one entry: **Fieldcraft**. The Fieldcraft surface owns progress, agenda, assessment and credentials, while the existing lifecycle tabs remain the technical curriculum map.

Do not create a parallel set of training-only scientific tabs. A lesson launches the learner into the real **Exploration**, **Field Development**, **Well Delivery**, **Drilling** or **Reservoir Management** workspace with a guided mission rail. When the task is complete, the learner returns to Fieldcraft with the observation, result or evidence reference captured.

On mobile, keep the five-slot navigation limit. Give **Learn** a direct bottom-navigation slot and move the lower-frequency Report destinations into a sheet reachable from Cockpit/Intelligence or a `More` action. If that shell change is too disruptive for V1, expose Learn in the Intelligence sheet temporarily, but treat that as a launch compromise.

Fieldcraft is a multi-course platform. Use five learner-facing global tabs and one role-gated platform tab. Course learning tools live inside the selected course rather than occupying global navigation.

## The learning spine

The platform hierarchy should be explicit and stable:

```text
Academy
└── Discipline
    └── Role profile
        └── Competency framework
            └── Proficiency target
                └── Pathway
                    └── Course
                        └── Module
                            └── Lesson
                                └── Activity / evidence / question
```

### Four proficiency levels

Align the visible ladder with the industry pattern while using language learners understand:

| Level | Learner label | Evidence standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | **Awareness** | Recognizes concepts, terminology, data types and workflow purpose |
| L2 | **Practitioner** | Executes a guided workflow and interprets expected results |
| L3 | **Advanced** | Selects methods, diagnoses uncertainty and defends a decision |
| L4 | **Expert** | Integrates disciplines, governs quality and mentors/reviews others |

Do not issue L3 or L4 based only on unsupervised MCQs. V1 can teach all levels, but formally credential L1 and guided L2. Later L3/L4 should require a reviewed work product or qualified assessor.

### The lesson rhythm

Every lesson follows one recognizable loop:

1. **Brief** — what decision the learner will make and why it matters.
2. **Learn** — concise concept block or existing presentation segment.
3. **See** — worked example grounded in Arganta knowledge/data.
4. **Do** — guided lab inside a real Arganta workbench.
5. **Decide** — scenario question using a plot, map, log, table or evidence card.
6. **Check** — 3–5 low-stakes MCQs with explanations and source links.
7. **Capture** — save one observation, artifact or decision to the learner portfolio.

This rhythm makes presentations one content block within training, not the product structure itself.

## Flagship program: The Volve Mission — From Discovery to Decision

### Audience and shape

- Audience: graduate geoscientists, early-career subsurface staff, data professionals entering upstream, and cross-discipline refreshers.
- Enterprise audience: asset teams, graduate programs, cross-discipline onboarding, national oil companies, service companies and university-industry cohorts.
- Level: L1 Awareness progressing into guided L2 Practitioner.
- Online effort: approximately 18–24 hours, self-paced.
- Live effort: one instructor-led package delivered over five consecutive 08:30–16:30 training days.
- Structure: 6 courses, 24 modules, approximately 70 short lessons, 18 guided labs, 24 module quizzes and one final exam.
- Assessment: all scored questions are multiple choice; labs supply the evidence/context for scenario MCQs.
- Credential: six lifecycle badges plus one **Integrated Geoscience Fieldcraft Passport — Foundation** certificate.

### Course series

| Course | Modules | Real Arganta grounding | Culminating learner decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. **Mission Briefing: Data, Knowledge & Evidence** | Volve context; lifecycle; source types; truth classes; data gaps | Cockpit, Data, Knowledge, Discovery report/PUD, provenance ledger | Approve the evidence pack that is fit to begin the field mission |
| 2. **Exploration: Find and Frame the Opportunity** | Existing Exploration tabs and subtabs: basin/play context, wells, interpretation, prospects, risk and volumes | Exploration workspaces, well evidence, formation tops, horizons and volumetrics | Decide whether the opportunity has enough evidence to progress |
| 3. **Field Development: Build the Field Case** | Existing Field Development tabs: map, logs, petrophysics, correlation, structure, properties, model, uncertainty, forecast and economics | Existing viewers plus petro, surface, volumetric, geostatistical, simulation, forecast and economic engines | Select a development case and state its key uncertainty |
| 4. **Well Delivery & Drilling: Turn the Plan into a Well** | Existing Well Delivery and Drilling tabs: basis, targets, trajectory, offset evidence, sequence, execution and learning | Trajectory components, well plans, drilling sequence, well evidence and handover views | Choose a well plan and identify the evidence needed at the next gate |
| 5. **Reservoir Management: Monitor, Diagnose and Act** | Existing Reservoir Management tabs: surveillance, production, injection/VRR, pressure, tests, patterns, forecast and opportunities | Production history, surveillance, DCA and opportunity-screening engines | Diagnose a field signal and select the next defensible action |
| 6. **Field Review: Integrate and Defend the Decision** | Cross-lifecycle synthesis, uncertainty, data gaps, decision quality and communication | Cockpit, Knowledge graph, evidence strips and Report/Document/Presentation outputs | Present the team's field recommendation and complete the final exam |

The lifecycle navigation is therefore both the product navigation and the curriculum map. The training layer supplies the sequence, learning objective, instructor cue, guided steps and assessment; the scientific surface remains the same one used in the app.

## Dual-delivery operating model

### One source, two experiences

Do not maintain separate online and classroom courseware. Every module is assembled from the same versioned objects:

```text
learning objectives
+ concept/presentation blocks
+ lifecycle deep links and saved view state
+ instructor demonstrations
+ individual or team labs
+ discussion prompts
+ MCQ pools
+ evidence references
+ timing and facilitation notes
```

### Recommended 5-day enterprise agenda

| Day | Theme | App spine | Live outputs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Discover** | Mission Briefing + Data + Knowledge + Exploration | Evidence-quality assessment and opportunity gate |
| 2 | **Describe & Design** | Field Development: wells/logs/structure → volume/uncertainty/value | Selected development case with uncertainty statement |
| 3 | **Deliver** | Well Delivery + Drilling | Well-gate and executable-sequence decision |
| 4 | **Operate** | Reservoir Management | Performance diagnosis and action recommendation |
| 5 | **Decide** | Cross-lifecycle field review + team presentation + final exam | Team field recommendation, individual exam and credential |

Days 1–4 use a consistent enterprise rhythm inside the eight-hour window: **27% theory/mission framing, 15% instructor demonstration, 35% hands-on work, 12% team decision challenge, 4% scored assessment and 7% guided review/transition within the sessions**. Breaks and lunch occupy 90 minutes, leaving 6.5 active learning hours.

### Locked launch format: five days

The current product scope is the **five-day instructor-led course only**. Three-day and four-day derivatives are explicitly deferred until the full course has been delivered, measured and improved. They must not influence the first syllabus or cause the five-day experience to become overpacked.

The detailed approved working syllabus is maintained in [FIELDCRAFT-VOLVE-5-DAY-SYLLABUS.md](./FIELDCRAFT-VOLVE-5-DAY-SYLLABUS.md).

### Instructor and enterprise mode

The role-gated Studio needs a **Live Cohort** workspace containing:

- cohort setup, client branding and participant import;
- five-day agenda builder from approved modules;
- facilitator guide and speaker notes;
- projector/presentation mode;
- launch-to-workspace demonstrations with fixed view states;
- session timer, attendance and completion overrides with an audit reason;
- live MCQ polls and anonymous confidence checks;
- team assignment and breakout mission controls;
- instructor unlocks for labs, answer review and final exam;
- room-level progress heatmap and misconception alerts;
- downloadable learner workbook and instructor guide;
- end-of-course report for the client: attendance, completion, competencies, scores and recommendations;
- certificate issuance and revocation controls.

### Offline and restricted-network deployment

“Offline” should support both **in-person delivery** and **no public internet**, which is common in enterprise environments.

Package each published course version as a signed **Fieldcraft Training Pack** containing the required frontend build, approved Volve training dataset, knowledge notes, presentation/media assets, lab view states, question pools and source manifests. It can run on one instructor laptop or an on-premise/LAN server for the classroom.

Requirements:

- no CDN, streaming or cloud-auth dependency for required activities;
- service worker/local cache for single-device mode;
- LAN cohort server for multi-learner mode;
- local progress and attempt event queue;
- encrypted learner export and later sync when permitted;
- deterministic conflict handling based on event IDs and course versions;
- no credential marked publicly verifiable until the signed completion record reaches the issuer;
- client data never bundled unless the client explicitly approves the training snapshot;
- training pack expiry/revocation and checksum verification.

### Reuse contract for the existing app

The training system may add orchestration but must not fork scientific logic:

- `WorkbenchDeepLink` opens an existing route, tab, scope, selected entities and saved view state.
- `GuidedMissionRail` overlays objective, steps, hints and completion criteria without replacing the workspace.
- `EngineChallenge` invokes the existing deterministic engine with a versioned training input pack.
- `EvidenceCapture` stores the selected source IDs, view state and learner conclusion.
- `ReturnToFieldcraft` brings the captured result back to the lesson or live cohort.
- Engine updates are inherited automatically after compatibility checks; published courses stay pinned until revalidated.

This makes Fieldcraft a demonstration layer, guided-practice layer and assessment layer over ArgantaEnergy—not a second application to maintain.

### What is available now

The current build already provides a credible foundation:

- 81 generated knowledge notes: 1 field, 11 wells, 24 wellbores, 16 surfaces, 10 datatables, 2 documents, 8 QC notes and 9 archaeology notes.
- 1,002 evidence-ledger entries in the baked data bundle.
- governed production, trajectory, horizon and well-related data in `foundation.json` and the workbench bundles;
- deterministic engines for volumetrics, uncertainty, decline, economics, geostatistics and surveillance;
- Exploration, Field Development, Well Delivery, Drilling and Reservoir Management workspaces;
- Report, Document and Presentation outputs;
- explicit data-nature/provenance conventions and source hashes.

### Honest launch constraints

- The local mirror intentionally excludes the large seismic volumes. Do not write labs that pretend full seismic interpretation is present.
- Geophysical depth interpretations can support surface/horizon exercises if included and validated, but they are not raw seismic.
- Some development-phase streams are absent. Missing 4D seismic, PLT or discrete well-test evidence must be taught as a data gap, not synthesized as fact.
- L3/L4 competency claims require a reviewer/assessor workflow that is not in V1.

These gaps are educational assets: learners should practice recognizing when a decision is under-supported.

## Assessment system

### Assessment ladder

| Layer | Purpose | Recommended rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnostic | Place the learner and recommend a path | 25–35 questions; ungraded; confidence requested after each answer |
| Lesson check | Retrieval and immediate correction | 3–5 questions; unlimited attempts; explanations always shown |
| Module quiz | Confirm local mastery | 10 questions drawn from a versioned pool; 80% pass; 3 attempts before remediation |
| Course checkpoint | Integrate several modules | 15 scenario MCQs; 80% pass; incorrect competency areas reopen |
| Final exam | Certify pathway knowledge | 50 MCQs; 75 minutes; 80% pass; 2 attempts, then remediation/cooldown |

### Question design standard

Use four-option, single-best-answer MCQs by default. Every scored item must have:

- one unambiguously best answer;
- a specific competency and cognitive level;
- a rationale for every option, not only the correct one;
- at least one content or evidence reference;
- an immutable source/content version;
- a difficulty and discrimination target;
- a reviewer and approval state;
- accessibility text for every visual;
- no answer that depends on hidden precision or unavailable data.

Target mix for module and final assessments:

- 20% terminology/concept recognition;
- 35% interpretation of a real plot, map, log, table or evidence card;
- 30% workflow/method selection;
- 15% uncertainty, provenance, QC and decision ethics.

Randomize from pools by competency and difficulty, not by arbitrary question count. Preserve the assessment blueprint across attempts.

### Certificate rules

Issue the pathway certificate only when all conditions are true:

- all required lessons and labs completed;
- all six course checkpoints passed at 80% or higher;
- final exam passed at 80% or higher;
- learner accepts an integrity statement;
- credential record stores course version, assessment version, score, issue date and status.

The downloadable PDF should show learner name, credential title, issue date, unique ID, QR verification URL, issuer and skills. The canonical credential should be a signed record prepared for Open Badges 3.0; the PDF is a human-readable rendering.

## Grounding contract

Every instructional assertion that presents a field fact must resolve through a grounding reference:

```ts
type GroundingRef = {
  refType: 'vault-note' | 'ledger-source' | 'data-row' | 'engine-result' | 'app-view';
  refId: string;
  locator?: string;          // page, sheet, row key, curve/depth, chart range, view state
  sha256?: string;           // source content hash where available
  dataNature: 'measured' | 'reported' | 'interpreted' | 'derived' | 'forecast' | 'scenario';
  validTime?: string;
  version: string;
};
```

Rules:

1. No grounded claim without at least one `GroundingRef`.
2. Generated prose is draft until expert-approved.
3. Answer explanations show a learner-friendly evidence link.
4. Published course versions pin their references; source changes create a content-health warning, not silent mutation.
5. Questions with stale, missing or conflicting evidence are automatically suspended from scored pools.
6. A certificate always points to the immutable pathway and assessment versions used.

Important architectural choices:

- Content is **config/data**, not hard-coded page markup.
- Courses use immutable published versions plus editable drafts.
- One lesson can be reused across pathways without duplicating it.
- Competencies and evidence references are many-to-many.
- Assessment pools are separate from lesson presentation order.
- Presentation, video, Markdown, chart, map, table, lab and quiz are block types under one lesson contract.
- Online and instructor-led variants reference the same module and assessment versions.
- Lifecycle labs store route, tab, scope, selected entities and view state rather than duplicating a workbench.
- Progress uses append-only events plus derived snapshots so completion can be audited.
- Training packs contain only approved, checksum-verified content and data snapshots.
- SCORM/xAPI import/export can be added later; do not make either the internal model.

## Authoring and governance workflow

```text
Source enters Data / Knowledge
→ content candidate generated or authored
→ grounding references attached
→ domain expert review
→ assessment review
→ accessibility / editorial QA
→ immutable course version published
→ learner attempts recorded
→ analytics and item-quality review
→ revision creates a new version
```

Required roles:

- Learner
- Instructor/facilitator
- Author
- Domain reviewer
- Assessment reviewer
- Publisher/admin
- Qualified assessor (later, for L3/L4)
- Organization manager (aggregate competency and cohort reporting only)

AI may propose outlines, summaries, distractors and remediation, but may not publish content, mark field judgment correct, or issue a credential without passing deterministic rules and human-approved material.

## Engagement without gimmicks

Use:

- a visible field investigation that develops across the pathway;
- short lessons with a consistent rhythm;
- real maps, logs, plots and evidence choices;
- immediate explanatory feedback in practice;
- competency progress rather than XP as the main score;
- optional streaks and weekly goals;
- meaningful course badges;
- a final field synthesis the learner can retain in the evidence portfolio.

Avoid:

- points for opening a page;
- decorative leaderboards for professional competency;
- fake urgency;
- overlong narrated slide decks;
- answer explanations that merely repeat the correct option;
- certificates based on video watch time.

- Role diagnostics and adaptive paths.
- Additional disciplines and customer-specific competency maps.
- Team dashboards and HR/LMS interfaces.
- Open Badges 3.0 / CLR 2.0 issuance.
- Mentoring, reviewed capstones and L3/L4 assessor workflow.
- Multilingual content and enterprise tenancy.
- Client-branded cohorts, SSO/HRIS integration and on-premise deployment support.

## Measures that matter

North-star metric: **verified competencies demonstrated per active learner**, not watch time.

Launch measures:

- diagnostic-to-first-lesson conversion;
- first-module completion;
- pathway completion;
- median time between sessions;
- first-attempt versus post-remediation mastery;
- question quality: facility, discrimination and distractor performance;
- evidence-link open rate;
- lab completion and artifact capture;
- certificate verification/share rate;
- stale-content and suspended-question count;
- learner confidence calibration before/after the pathway.
- instructor preparation time and agenda adherence;
- cohort lab completion and misconception hotspots;
- offline-pack integrity and sync success;
- enterprise client reuse rate across cohorts.

1. Approve the proposed brand: **Arganta Fieldcraft**, **Fieldcraft Passport**, and **The Volve Mission: From Discovery to Decision**.

4. Confirm the proposed 80% pass mark, 50-question final and two final-exam attempts.
5. Name the domain expert(s) who can approve geology, petrophysics and integrated field questions.
6. Decide whether the first public certificate is Arganta-issued only or co-endorsed by a training/industry partner later.
7. Confirm the five-day syllabus, 08:30–16:30 daily schedule and 100-point daily team competition.
8. Confirm whether V1 offline delivery must support one instructor device only or a multi-learner local-network server.

## Recommendation in one sentence

Build one Fieldcraft system that works self-paced or live, uses the existing lifecycle workspaces as its laboratory, and turns every learner decision into traceable evidence for an enterprise-ready Passport.
