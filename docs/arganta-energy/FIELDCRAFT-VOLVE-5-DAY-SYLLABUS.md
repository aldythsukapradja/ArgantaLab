# ARGANTA FIELDCRAFT

## The Volve Mission: From Discovery to Decision

Five-day instructor-led enterprise syllabus  
Credential: **Integrated Geoscience Fieldcraft Passport — Foundation**  
Tagline: **One field. Every lifecycle. Decisions proven by evidence.**  
Status: syllabus proposal for founder and domain-expert review  
Date: 2026-08-02

This is the flagship course syllabus inside the wider [Arganta Fieldcraft training platform](./FIELDCRAFT-PLATFORM-BUILD-PLAN.md).

## 1. Course promise

Participants follow one real North Sea field through the upstream lifecycle. They do not learn from slides alone: they inspect governed Volve evidence, operate the existing ArgantaEnergy lifecycle workspaces, use the existing deterministic engines, make team decisions and defend those decisions with traceable evidence.

The five-day story is:

```text
DAY 1 · DISCOVER        Exploration
DAY 2 · DESCRIBE/DESIGN Field Development
DAY 3 · DELIVER         Well Delivery & Drilling
DAY 4 · OPERATE         Reservoir Management
DAY 5 · DECIDE          Integrated Field Review
```

The course is deliberately selective. It teaches one coherent decision thread through each lifecycle rather than attempting to demonstrate every tab, method and engine. Additional tabs remain available for follow-up practice and future advanced courses.

## 2. Audience, level and prerequisites

### Intended audience

- graduate and early-career geoscientists;
- subsurface data professionals entering upstream workflows;
- petroleum engineers and well professionals needing cross-discipline context;
- experienced staff completing an integrated lifecycle refresher;
- enterprise graduate programs and multidisciplinary asset teams.

### Level

- Starts at **L1 Awareness**.
- Finishes at **guided L2 Practitioner** for the selected workflows.
- Does not claim unsupervised L3/L4 professional competence.

### Prerequisites

- Basic geology or petroleum-industry awareness is helpful but not mandatory.
- Participants must be comfortable reading simple maps, charts and tables.
- No Petrel, Techlog, OFM or programming experience is required.
- Required pre-course orientation: 30 minutes covering login, navigation, units, depth references, evidence badges and the training integrity statement.

## 3. Delivery architecture

### Daily time envelope

Training runs **08:30–16:30**, eight clock hours per day.

- Active instruction and practice: **6 hours 30 minutes**.
- Morning break: **15 minutes**.
- Lunch: **60 minutes**.
- Afternoon break: **15 minutes**.

The normal day uses the same rhythm so participants always know where they are:

| Time | Duration | Mode | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 08:30–09:00 | 30 min | Mission launch | Review, objectives, scenario and team plan |
| 09:00–10:00 | 60 min | Theory | Essential concepts only |
| 10:00–10:15 | 15 min | Break | — |
| 10:15–11:15 | 60 min | Instructor demonstration | One end-to-end workflow in the real app |
| 11:15–12:30 | 75 min | Guided hands-on lab | Individual work with coaching |
| 12:30–13:30 | 60 min | Lunch | — |
| 13:30–14:15 | 45 min | Applied theory | Decision method, uncertainty and common pitfalls |
| 14:15–15:15 | 60 min | Team lab | Apply the method and produce a decision artifact |
| 15:15–15:30 | 15 min | Break | — |
| 15:30–16:15 | 45 min | Fieldcraft Challenge | Team decision and short defense |
| 16:15–16:30 | 15 min | Daily check and debrief | 10 MCQs, reveal leaderboard, preview next day |

Day 5 uses a modified schedule for the integrated capstone, team Field Reviews and 75-minute final exam.

### Learning-mode balance

Across Days 1–4:

- 27% theory and mission framing;
- 15% instructor demonstration;
- 35% individual/team hands-on work;
- 12% team challenge and decision defense;
- 4% scored assessment;
- 7% guided review, transition and feedback within the blocks.

This prevents slide fatigue while leaving enough conceptual foundation for participants who are new to a lifecycle stage.

## 4. Competition and scoring system

### Team structure

- Stable multidisciplinary teams of **3–5 participants**.
- Recommended cohort: **12–24 participants**, producing 3–6 teams.
- Teams choose a name and rotate four roles: Navigator, Analyst, Evidence Lead and Decision Lead. A fifth participant acts as Challenger/QA.
- Roles rotate daily so one experienced user cannot operate the app for the whole team.

### Daily Fieldcraft Score — 100 points

| Dimension | Points | Scoring principle |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Technical workflow and result | 40 | Correct use of the selected app components/engine; reproducible output; appropriate QC |
| Evidence and provenance | 20 | Claims linked to valid evidence; truth classes handled correctly; gaps stated honestly |
| Decision quality | 20 | Recommendation answers the mission question and reflects uncertainty/trade-offs |
| Individual daily quiz average | 15 | Team mean across the ten individual MCQs |
| Communication and team operation | 5 | Clear short defense, role rotation and inclusive participation |
| **Total** | **100** | — |

No points are awarded for finishing first. Speed is used only to ensure completion within the session. The primary tiebreaker is **fewest unsupported claims**; the secondary tiebreaker is the higher individual quiz average.

### Score progression

- One **Lifecycle Flag** is awarded to the top team each day.
- The cumulative board totals **500 possible points** over five days.
- The board shows team scores; individual assessment results remain private.
- The final winning team receives the **Volve Fieldcraft Cup**.
- Competition results do not determine individual certification.

### Credential rules

Each participant must independently:

- attend at least 90% of active course time;
- complete all four lifecycle labs and the integrated capstone contribution;
- achieve a combined daily-quiz average of at least 80%;
- pass the 50-question final exam at 80% or higher;
- accept the integrity statement.

A participant who does not pass receives a completion record and a targeted remediation plan, not the Passport credential.

## 5. Day 1 — DISCOVER

### Exploration: Can this opportunity progress?

**Mission question:** What do we know, how do we know it, and is the opportunity mature enough to progress?

### Learning outcomes

By the end of Day 1, participants can:

1. Explain the relationship between basin, play, lead, prospect, discovery and field.
2. Describe source, reservoir, seal, trap and timing at an awareness level.
3. Distinguish measured, reported, interpreted, derived, forecast and scenario information.
4. Navigate the Arganta Data, Knowledge and Exploration surfaces.
5. Read a simple evidence-backed risk and volumetric case.
6. Recommend **Progress, Study or Stop** without hiding a material data gap.

### Detailed agenda

| Time | Session | Content and activity | Arganta surface/output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 08:30–09:00 | **Mission launch** | Welcome; Volve story; five-day decision thread; team formation; baseline confidence poll | Cockpit; cohort board; mission dossier opened |
| 09:00–10:00 | **Theory 1 — Exploration mental model** | Lifecycle vocabulary; petroleum-system elements; play-to-prospect maturation; evidence versus interpretation; why uncertainty is not error | Presentation + live evidence cards |
| 10:15–11:15 | **Instructor demo — Follow the evidence** | Move from Cockpit → Data inventory → Knowledge note/graph → Exploration; inspect provenance; compare supported and unsupported statements | Saved evidence trail with source IDs and truth classes |
| 11:15–12:30 | **Lab 1 — Build the evidence pack** | Individuals inspect a curated Volve evidence set, classify each item, identify one conflict/gap and select the evidence fit for an exploration brief | `EvidencePack` artifact |
| 13:30–14:15 | **Theory 2 — Risk and volume without false precision** | Chance factors; dependency warning; deterministic inputs; P90/P50/P10 convention; difference between uncertainty range and chance of success | Risk/volume worked example |
| 14:15–15:15 | **Lab 2 — Screen the opportunity** | Teams review a prepared prospect case, test a volumetric/risk scenario using existing engines and record the highest-impact uncertainty | `ExplorationScreen` artifact |
| 15:30–16:15 | **Challenge — Progress, Study or Stop?** | Each team submits one decision, three supporting evidence references, one material gap and the next best action; three-minute defense plus challenge question | Day 1 decision card |
| 16:15–16:30 | **Daily check** | 10 individual MCQs; feedback on common misconception; Lifecycle Flag | Day 1 score / remediation tags |

### Hands-on boundaries

- Participants do not perform raw seismic interpretation because the training bundle does not contain the full seismic volume.
- Horizon and formation evidence may be reviewed as interpreted products.
- The purpose is evidence maturity, risk and decision framing—not mastery of basin modeling or seismic processing.

### Day 1 quiz blueprint

- 2 questions: lifecycle and petroleum-system concepts;
- 3 questions: evidence type and provenance;
- 2 questions: risk versus uncertainty;
- 2 questions: interpreting the lab's table/map/result;
- 1 question: appropriate next action when evidence is incomplete.

### Competition deliverable

**Exploration Gate Card:** decision, evidence, uncertainty, next action and one-sentence rationale.

## 6. Day 2 — DESCRIBE & DESIGN

### Field Development: What reservoir are we developing, and which case should move forward?

**Mission question:** Which subsurface/development case is technically defensible and decision-relevant?

### Learning outcomes

By the end of Day 2, participants can:

1. Connect wells, logs, markers, surfaces, properties and contacts into an integrated static picture.
2. Explain the purpose of basic petrophysical interpretation, correlation and structural modeling.
3. Trace a volume result back to its geometry and property assumptions.
4. Interpret a simple uncertainty distribution and identify the dominant driver.
5. Compare development cases using forecast and economic consequences at an awareness level.
6. Select a case while separating observed evidence from modeled assumptions.

### Detailed agenda

| Time | Session | Content and activity | Arganta surface/output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 08:30–09:00 | **Mission launch** | Day 1 recap; exploration decision handed to the development team; role rotation; field-development question | Day 1 Gate Card loaded into field dossier |
| 09:00–10:00 | **Theory 1 — From wells to a reservoir model** | Well/wellbore identity; MD/TVD/TVDSS; logs and petrophysical properties; correlation; surfaces/contacts; grid/property concepts; model purpose and limits | Layered model storyboard |
| 10:15–11:15 | **Instructor demo — Evidence to volume** | Navigate Map → Logs/Petrophysics → Correlation → Structure/Properties → Volumetrics; demonstrate how one input change travels into the result | Reproducible base-case lineage |
| 11:15–12:30 | **Lab 1 — QC the field case** | Individuals inspect selected wells/logs/surfaces, find planted QC issues or ambiguities, approve/reject inputs and run a base volume case | `SubsurfaceCaseReview` artifact |
| 13:30–14:15 | **Theory 2 — Uncertainty, forecast and value** | Parameter ranges and correlations; deterministic versus probabilistic cases; sensitivity; screening forecast; economics as a decision bridge, not geological truth | Case-comparison worked example |
| 14:15–15:15 | **Lab 2 — Compare development cases** | Teams compare two prepared cases, run uncertainty/forecast/economic views, identify the value driver and state what the model cannot support | `DevelopmentCaseComparison` artifact |
| 15:30–16:15 | **Challenge — Select the development case** | Recommend Case A, Case B or Rework; cite three inputs/results; name the dominant uncertainty; propose one value-of-information action | Day 2 development decision card |
| 16:15–16:30 | **Daily check** | 10 individual MCQs; misconception review; Lifecycle Flag | Day 2 score / remediation tags |

### Scope control

This day does not attempt full petrophysics, geostatistics, reservoir simulation and economics training. Participants see how these components connect and deeply operate one traceable volume/uncertainty decision thread. Advanced methods become separate future Fieldcraft passports.

### Day 2 quiz blueprint

- 2 questions: depth references, wells/logs and model elements;
- 2 questions: correlation, surfaces and contacts;
- 3 questions: volume lineage and uncertainty;
- 2 questions: interpreting the lab output;
- 1 question: selecting a justified value-of-information action.

### Competition deliverable

**Development Case Card:** selected case, key assumptions, uncertainty driver, value consequence and recommended next study.

## 7. Day 3 — DELIVER

### Well Delivery & Drilling: How do we turn the selected case into an executable well?

**Mission question:** Is the proposed well technically ready, safely framed and schedulable?

### Learning outcomes

By the end of Day 3, participants can:

1. Translate a development objective into targets and well success criteria.
2. Read a basic well trajectory in MD, TVD and TVDSS and recognize dogleg/clearance concerns.
3. Use offset evidence to identify drilling and geological hazards.
4. Explain the relationship between basis, design, risk, readiness and approval gates.
5. Read a rig-by-time sequence and identify a conflict or readiness constraint.
6. Recommend **Approve, Condition or Hold** for a well-delivery gate.

### Detailed agenda

| Time | Session | Content and activity | Arganta surface/output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 08:30–09:00 | **Mission launch** | Day 2 case becomes an approved development intent; introduce candidate well and success criteria; rotate roles | Development Case Card linked to well mission |
| 09:00–10:00 | **Theory 1 — Well maturation and design** | Objective/SOR/BOD; target definition; trajectory terms; casing/completion intent; offset learning; geological/operational risk; approval gates | Well maturation storyboard |
| 10:15–11:15 | **Instructor demo — Follow one well** | Open Well Delivery; move from basis/target to trajectory, offset evidence, risk and handover; show explicit depth references and evidence | Versioned well basis and trajectory review |
| 11:15–12:30 | **Lab 1 — Review the well plan** | Individuals test the target/trajectory against a prepared checklist, inspect offsets and identify one design conflict and one missing approval item | `WellPlanReview` artifact |
| 13:30–14:15 | **Theory 2 — From one well to an executable campaign** | Rig capacity; duration and dependencies; readiness; schedule versus sequence; revision control; why a technically sound well can still be undeliverable | Schedule logic worked example |
| 14:15–15:15 | **Lab 2 — Protect the slot** | Teams place/review the well in the drilling sequence, detect a conflict, compare two revisions and choose a controlled resolution | `WellReadinessAndSequence` artifact |
| 15:30–16:15 | **Challenge — Approve, Condition or Hold?** | Gate decision with objective, key hazard, mitigating condition, schedule impact and required evidence; three-minute defense | Day 3 well-gate card |
| 16:15–16:30 | **Daily check** | 10 individual MCQs; misconception review; Lifecycle Flag | Day 3 score / remediation tags |

### Scope control

The course does not teach detailed torque-and-drag, hydraulics, casing design, well control or completion engineering. It teaches the integrated well-decision spine and the ability to recognize when specialist analysis is required.

### Day 3 quiz blueprint

- 2 questions: well objectives, targets and gates;
- 2 questions: MD/TVD/TVDSS and trajectory interpretation;
- 2 questions: offset evidence and risk;
- 2 questions: sequencing/readiness and revisions;
- 2 questions: interpreting the lab's trajectory or schedule view.

### Competition deliverable

**Well Gate Card:** gate status, evidence, hazard, condition/mitigation and schedule consequence.

## 8. Day 4 — OPERATE

### Reservoir Management: What changed, why, and what action creates value?

**Mission question:** Which observed field signal matters, what is the most defensible diagnosis and what should the asset team do next?

### Learning outcomes

By the end of Day 4, participants can:

1. Apply the monitor → diagnose → forecast → act → learn loop.
2. Read production rate, cumulative, water-cut/GOR and uptime trends.
3. Distinguish observation, diagnosis and recommended action.
4. Explain the purpose and limitation of pressure, VRR, well-test and surveillance evidence.
5. Create and compare a screening decline forecast.
6. Rank an opportunity using technical evidence, uncertainty and value.

### Detailed agenda

| Time | Session | Content and activity | Arganta surface/output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 08:30–09:00 | **Mission launch** | Move from delivered well to producing field; review Day 3 conditions; introduce performance exception; rotate roles | Well Gate Card linked to operating dossier |
| 09:00–10:00 | **Theory 1 — Surveillance and diagnosis** | Reservoir-management loop; production/injection data; rates and ratios; pressure/VRR; surveillance coverage; signal versus mechanism; honest no-data states | Surveillance decision loop |
| 10:15–11:15 | **Instructor demo — Monitor to diagnosis** | Navigate Overview/Surveillance → Production → Pressure/Patterns; inspect exceptions and show how a diagnosis record retains evidence and confidence | Grounded performance diagnosis |
| 11:15–12:30 | **Lab 1 — Diagnose the exception** | Individuals examine a selected well/pattern, validate the signal, compare peers, choose the most defensible mechanism and request the next measurement | `PerformanceDiagnosis` artifact |
| 13:30–14:15 | **Theory 2 — Forecast, opportunity and closed-loop learning** | DCA purpose/limits; baseline and scenario; intervention uplift; screening value; action tracking; outcome verification | Forecast/action worked example |
| 14:15–15:15 | **Lab 2 — Screen the action** | Teams run a base forecast and one intervention scenario, compare uncertainty/value and rank Act, Acquire Data or Monitor | `ReservoirActionScreen` artifact |
| 15:30–16:15 | **Challenge — Act, Acquire Data or Monitor?** | Submit diagnosis, confidence, action, expected signal, success measure and review date; three-minute defense | Day 4 action card |
| 16:15–16:30 | **Daily check** | 10 individual MCQs; misconception review; Lifecycle Flag | Day 4 score / remediation tags |

### Honest-data boundary

Where Volve lacks 4D seismic, PLT or discrete transient-test evidence, the correct learning outcome may be to request data or reduce confidence. The course must never turn an absent stream into a simulated fact unless it is visibly labeled as a training scenario.

### Day 4 quiz blueprint

- 2 questions: monitor/diagnose/act workflow;
- 3 questions: interpreting production/surveillance plots;
- 2 questions: pressure, VRR and data limitations;
- 2 questions: forecast and opportunity screening;
- 1 question: defining a measurable action outcome.

### Competition deliverable

**Reservoir Action Card:** observation, diagnosis, confidence, action, expected value/signal and verification plan.

## 9. Day 5 — DECIDE

### Integrated Field Review: What should happen next?

**Mission question:** Considering the whole evidence trail, what is the next best field decision and why?

### Learning outcomes

By the end of Day 5, participants can:

1. Trace how an assumption or data gap propagates across lifecycle decisions.
2. Reconcile exploration, development, well-delivery and operating perspectives.
3. Separate facts, interpretations, forecasts and recommendations in a decision brief.
4. Prioritize actions using value, risk, readiness and evidence maturity.
5. Deliver a concise, evidence-backed Field Review and respond to challenge questions.
6. Demonstrate individual foundation-level mastery in the final exam.

### Modified Day 5 agenda

| Time | Duration | Session | Content and output |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 08:30–09:00 | 30 min | **Final mission launch** | Reveal the integrated field scenario, scoring rubric and decision question; assign final team roles |
| 09:00–10:00 | 60 min | **Theory — Integrated decision quality** | Decision framing; evidence chain; uncertainty propagation; option comparison; recommendation versus approval; concise technical storytelling |
| 10:00–10:15 | 15 min | **Break** | — |
| 10:15–12:30 | 135 min | **Integrated capstone build — Part 1** | Teams use the existing lifecycle workspaces, previous artifacts and one new event card to build their final recommendation |
| 12:30–13:30 | 60 min | **Lunch** | — |
| 13:30–13:45 | 15 min | **Integrated capstone build — Part 2** | Final evidence check, submit decision artifact and freeze team deck/view state |
| 13:45–15:00 | 75 min | **The Field Review** | Up to six teams: 7-minute presentation + 3-minute panel challenge; remaining time for scoring calibration, cumulative leaderboard and Fieldcraft Cup reveal |

For cohorts larger than six teams, run two review panels in parallel or extend the course day. Do not reduce each team's defense below ten minutes.

### Integrated capstone brief

Each team receives one new material event—for example a changed surface interpretation, a trajectory constraint, a production exception or a revised economic assumption. The team must determine whether that event changes the current field decision.

The final recommendation must contain exactly seven elements:

1. decision requested;
2. recommended option;
3. three decisive evidence references;
4. top two uncertainties;
5. lifecycle consequences;
6. next action, owner and timing;
7. success measure or decision-revisit trigger.

| Dimension | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| Cross-lifecycle technical coherence | 25 |
| Evidence chain and provenance | 20 |
| Decision quality and uncertainty treatment | 30 |
| Field Review clarity and challenge response | 25 |
| **Total** | **100** |

The individual final exam is excluded from team competition scoring. This keeps the credential assessment private and prevents one participant's exam performance from advantaging or disadvantaging colleagues.

### Final exam blueprint — 50 MCQs

| Domain | Questions |
| --- | ---: |
| Data, knowledge, provenance and truth classes | 7 |
| Exploration and opportunity framing | 9 |
| Field development, models, volume and uncertainty | 12 |
| Well delivery, trajectory, risk and schedule | 9 |
| Reservoir management, forecast and opportunities | 9 |
| Integrated decision quality | 4 |
| **Total** | **50** |

At least 60% of final-exam questions must use a field artifact: map, log, chart, table, evidence card, trajectory, schedule, distribution or scenario excerpt. Pure terminology recall must remain below 20%.

## 11. Instructor package

The approved training release must include:

- master instructor guide with learning objectives, timing, transitions and answer keys;
- one concise presentation sequence per day;
- demonstration scripts with exact app route, scope, selected entities and view state;
- learner workbook with mission briefs and observation templates;
- Volve training dataset and immutable evidence/source manifest;
- prepared base cases and planted issues for labs;
- team challenge cards and scoring rubrics;
- 40 daily-quiz questions plus alternates;
- versioned final-exam bank and blueprint;
- facilitator recovery notes for app/data problems;
- projector mode and cohort scoreboard;
- printable fallback artifacts for every required visual;
- certificate and completion-report templates.

### Instructor staffing

- Up to 16 participants: one lead instructor plus one technical facilitator.
- 17–24 participants: one lead instructor plus two facilitators.
- The lead should be able to facilitate integrated decisions; specialist questions can be routed to reviewed reference notes rather than improvised answers.

## 12. Room and technical setup

- One laptop per participant; two large displays or one display plus confidence monitor.
- Teams seated together but every learner operates the app during individual labs.
- Instructor machine uses the same signed course/data version as learners.
- Public-internet-free delivery uses the approved Fieldcraft Training Pack on a local server/LAN.
- Complete a technical rehearsal and data-integrity check one working day before delivery.
- Freeze automatic application updates during the course.
- Capture progress locally and sync after the course only when client policy permits.

## 13. Course quality gates

The syllabus is ready for delivery only when:

- every lab has been completed by a novice tester within 80% of the allocated time;
- every demo has a saved start state and verified fallback;
- all field claims resolve to approved grounding references;
- every scored MCQ has a reviewed rationale for all four options;
- no question depends on missing seismic or other unavailable evidence;
- the 50-question exam matches its blueprint and has no duplicate learning objective concentration;
- the full five-day timing has been rehearsed without using lunch or breaks as recovery time;
- accessibility and keyboard operation are verified for required tasks;
- credential, attendance and remediation rules are communicated before Day 1.

## 14. Items requiring domain-expert sign-off

1. Final selection of the Volve prospect/opportunity scenario used on Day 1.
2. Approved Day 2 volume case, uncertainty ranges and economic assumptions.
3. Approved Day 3 well objective, trajectory constraint and schedule conflict.
4. Approved Day 4 performance exception, diagnosis options and intervention response assumptions.
5. The new-event card set for the Day 5 capstone.
6. The 40 daily quiz questions, final-exam blueprint and full question bank.
7. Scoring rubrics and acceptable alternative interpretations.
