## Where the review itself has attackable weaknesses (the actual battle test)

### W1 — The ritual wedge is two-sided, and the review under-prices that
The recommended loop requires **simultaneous behavior change from parent AND child** before value fires. Two-sided activation roughly multiplies failure modes: a parent who loves the board with a kid who refuses the quest = no loop; an eager kid with a parent who never opens Today = no loop. Ollie/Cozi are one-sided; Duolingo is one-sided. **Mitigation to bake into the pilot:** the wedge must deliver *parent-only value on day one* (Today handled, whiteboard imported) so activation is single-sided, with the child quest introduced day 2–3 as the *retention* layer — and the two demand-test messages (logistics-led vs learning-led) measure not just acquisition but *which side activates first*. If the review's ritual is tested as an all-at-once bundle, a failed pilot won't tell you which half failed.

### W2 — "Evidence, not vanity XP" collides with the actual engine
The tested learning engine is a *game* — XP, diamonds, rings, ranks, cosmetics. The review's Progress surface demands mastery evidence ("what was attempted, accuracy trend"). Those aren't the same data, and the drills→mastery mapping is real work the review's 240-hour budget doesn't itemize. Risk: the pilot ships game-XP relabeled as "evidence," parents see through it, and the core differentiator tests false for tooling reasons, not thesis reasons. **Add: a minimal honest mastery model (per-skill accuracy over attempts) is in-scope for days 1–14, or the evidence claim is softened to "visible real activity" for cohort 1.**

### W3 — The review's own evidence has soft spots
It practices better claims discipline than I did, but: the decision-gate thresholds (≥60% 48h activation, D7 ≥30%) are still invented management heuristics (it admits this — good); the Duolingo/Ollie/Cozi citations are load-bearing and dated 2025–26 (verify links before quoting to investors); and its **12-month scenario model omits the founder's actual cash situation** — day-job income, Qatar cost base, AI-quota budget ([[18-Remaining-Success-Gaps]] S12) — the very "full-company cash model" it demands is still nobody's deliverable with a date. The Opus handoff assigns it.

### W4 — It discards the emotional/brand layer too lightly
The review compresses brand to §12's instrumented moments (excellent) but silently drops: the F9 doctrine reconciliation ([[16-Brand-GroundTruth-and-Exemplars]] — desire-map collapse, dual-metaphor ruling, claims classes going public), the logo problem ([[15-Logo-Design-Audit]] — the AI-gradient icon actively contradicts the warm positioning the review endorses), and the social-exemplar playbook. These survive and transfer into the handoff — a pilot family's first touch is still a landing page wearing the wrong clothes.

### W5 — Qatar/partnership layer is absent
The review says "Indonesian/Qatar family and school-community networks" once, but the non-dilutive funding map (QSTP/QDB/WISE), the zero-retention API terms task, startup-credit programs, and the operator-angel sequencing of [[21-Monetization-Partners-Investors]] appear nowhere. For a founder whose runway math is undefined (W3), *free money and credits are material*, not garnish. Transfers into the handoff.

### W6 — One strategic risk the review creates: modesty as a ceiling
"Do not promise a family OS / memory moat / $1B" is right for *marketing and pilot honesty* — but an investor conversation still requires a credible expansion story ("if the ritual works, what makes this big?"). The review's answer is implicit (loop → household expansion → platform). The handoff must make Act-2 explicit as *labeled hypothesis*, or the pitch reads as a lifestyle business — over-correcting from my over-promising to under-claiming.
