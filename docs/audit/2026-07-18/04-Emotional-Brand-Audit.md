# Emotional & Brand Audit

You asked the right question: *"I want a lot of users to like it emotionally."* Products get used for utility and **kept** for feeling. Audit of where the feeling currently lives — and where it leaks.

## The one true emotional asset

The Customer Zero story is not marketing material; it is the *product thesis*:

- One parent (your wife) was the household's single point of failure — the whiteboard, the schedule, the memory.
- The other parent kept asking questions the system couldn't answer without her.
- The kids' learning was invisible until it became visible.
- The whiteboard became unnecessary.

The emotion here is **relief + being seen**. Specifically: the over-loaded parent feels *seen* ("someone finally noticed the invisible work"), and the under-informed parent feels *competent*. That's a Pixar-grade emotional pair, and it's true. Everything in [[01-Vision-Critique]] about launch video and messaging should be built on exactly this.

## Where the current repo's emotion actually points

Audit the aesthetics honestly: reactor cores, volumetric brains, Jarvis, agents, command bridges, cinematic WebGL decks. The repo's dominant emotion is **founder awe** — Iron Man fantasy. It is genuinely cool, and it is aimed at *you*. The wedge doc already diagnosed it: "NOT cyberpunk. NOT glowing AI brains. Warm. Minimal. Human." The repo is ~90% glowing brains.

This isn't a moral failing — it's a segmentation error. Keep the Jarvis aesthetic for HQ (your cockpit). The family product needs the opposite emotional temperature: morning-kitchen-light, fridge-door, crayon, "we've got today handled."

## Emotional moments the product must engineer (the "like it emotionally" answer)

Users love products at specific *moments*, not in general. Candidate signature moments for Arganta, ranked:

1. **The Sunday reset** — assistant summarizes the family's week ahead in 20 seconds; parent exhales. (Relief. Weekly ritual = retention.)
2. **"Ask Arganta, not Mama"** — child or spouse gets the answer without interrupting the load-bearing parent. (Being seen. This is the whiteboard story productized.)
3. **The learning reveal** — parent sees, unprompted, "Kayla mastered fractions this month" with real evidence from the kids' games. (Pride. No competitor can fake this; you own the whole loop.)
4. **The remembered detail** — assistant recalls something small ("swimming needs goggles today") that only the family's memory could know. (Feeling known — the Oasis memory-moat, but domestic.)
5. **Moments resurfacing** — "one year ago today" from family Moments. (Warmth. Cheap to build, deep to feel.)

Design the wedge so each of these fires in week one. That is what "emotional product" means operationally.

## Brand hierarchy (fixing [[03-Gap-Analysis]] G9)

- **Arganta** — the only public brand. Warm, human, few words.
- Workspaces are *features*: Family, Kids, Home. Not brands.
- KinQuest/Lashira characters survive as the *cast* of the Kids Workspace — Pixar has characters, not sub-brands.
- The five influencer personas: pause (already decided in persona canon — founder launches alone, real twin). Correct; hold that line.
- ArgantaLab/HQ/Core — internal names, never user-facing.

## Tone-of-voice one-liner set (seed for the manifesto action item)

- "Your family, remembered."
- "One parent shouldn't have to be the family's operating system."
- "Arganta wasn't built for a market. It was built for one family — mine."
